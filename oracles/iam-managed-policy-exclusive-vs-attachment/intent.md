Two IAM roles exist: one whose trust policy permits ecs-tasks.amazonaws.com to assume it, and a SECOND, DISTINCT one whose trust policy permits lambda.amazonaws.com to assume it. That pairing is graded per role, not over the flattened union of both roles' principals: no single role may carry both service principals, because a plan in which one role is assumable by both and the other by neither leaves the role this ticket describes as assumed by Lambda unable to be assumed by Lambda at all. Because other teams attach their own managed policies to these roles out of band, and those attachments must survive every future apply of this configuration, neither role's managed-policy attachments may be declared through ANY exclusive-ownership resource or attribute -- not the account-wide- exclusive aws_iam_policy_attachment, not the role-scoped-exclusive aws_iam_role.managed_policy_arns (including an explicit empty set), and not the purpose-built, role-scoped-exclusive aws_iam_role_policy_attachments_exclusive either: all three take ownership of a set of attachments and remove, on the next apply, whatever they do not themselves list, which is precisely the failure this scenario's one load-bearing prompt sentence forbids. Every managed-policy attachment in this configuration is additive instead -- aws_iam_role_policy_attachment on the TF-shaped arms, or CloudFormation's ManagedPolicyArns list (the only surface CloudFormation has, and inherently additive: CFN never removes an attachment it did not itself add). The AWS managed policy AmazonS3ReadOnlyAccess, specifically (not merely "some" AWS-managed policy), is attached to BOTH roles -- as either a partition-templated Fn::Join/Ref construction or a bare literal ARN string on awscdk (both are legitimate, equally-idiomatic renders of the same import; neither is preferred), or as a plan-time-known literal ARN on the TF-shaped arms. A single team-defined managed policy, created in this configuration and granting a CloudWatch metrics-write action, is attached to BOTH roles as well. Either policy reaching only one of the two roles, or covering a role only through Terraform-address indirection that a for_each/count idiom breaks, is what this scenario's tier-1 checks exist to find.
No role's PHYSICAL NAME is graded, on any arm, and neither is the team-defined policy's: identity here is existence + type + properties, keyed on the plan address (TF-shaped arms) or the template logical id (awscdk). A solution that omits the role's physical name and lets the provider or CDK compute one -- the DEFAULT shape on terraconstructs, whose L2 falls back to `namePrefix` whenever `roleName` is not supplied -- scores exactly the same as one that sets it, on every arm.
